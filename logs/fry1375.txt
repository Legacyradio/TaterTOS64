CORRECTED DIAGNOSIS — the real blocker is the JSC futex deadlock (matches the
prior handoff: "futex word transitions, 0x63ad6e0").
  0x63ad6e0 is a 3-state JSC mutex (0 free / 1 locked / 2 locked+waiters);
  threads FUTEX_WAIT while word==2 (a2=0x80 private-wait, val=2), unlock does
  FUTEX_WAKE 1 (a2=0x81). End-of-run state in BOTH boots: pid 3,4,5 all parked
  on 0x63ad6e0 with wake_time=infinity and no further wake -> hang.

KERNEL FUTEX PRIMITIVES ARE CORRECT (ruled out lost-wakeup in the kernel):
  - sched_block_futex (sched.c:530): under g_sched_lock, checks *word!=expected
    -> EAGAIN, else enqueues WAITING with wait_futex_key AND re-checks *word
    under the same lock (line 587). Atomic vs wake.
  - sched_wake_futex (sched.c:703): under g_sched_lock, wakes WAITING procs with
    matching key+bitset. Atomic vs block.
  - "futex-wake-miss" is benign: wake arrives before waiter enqueues; waiter
    then re-checks the word and returns EAGAIN instead of sleeping.

WHERE IT ACTUALLY GOES WRONG (next instrument)
  pid=3 was woken (L2193), took the lock, then FUTEX_WAKE'd at L2203 (= unlock).
  Yet pid 4/5 subsequently FUTEX_WAIT on word==2 and nobody releases. So the
  fault is in the lock-holder handoff / word-value transitions of 0x63ad6e0, not
  the kernel queue. Need a trace of EVERY write to the 0x63ad6e0 word (value
  before/after) + which thread currently holds the lock, to catch either:
    (a) a holder that exits/blocks-elsewhere without releasing, or
    (b) a word stuck at 2 when it should be 0 (a transition the unlocker missed).
